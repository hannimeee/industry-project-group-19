# MAST30034 Project 2 — Group 19

Merchant ranking and portfolio selection for a Buy Now, Pay Later provider. The project compares expected monthly fees, variation in weekly fees and shared-customer exposure to select an overall portfolio and portfolios for five merchant segments.

## Start here

- **Non-technical findings and recommendations:** [13_non_technical_summary.ipynb](code/notebooks/13_non_technical_summary.ipynb).
- **Technical explanation and saved rankings:** [12_summary.ipynb](code/notebooks/12_summary.ipynb).
- **Data preparation and quality:** [01_etl.ipynb](code/notebooks/01_etl.ipynb) and [02_analysis.ipynb](code/notebooks/02_analysis.ipynb).
- **Final portfolio files:** [fraud-adjusted exports](handoff/fraud_adjusted/final/) and [data dictionary](handoff/fraud_adjusted/final/data_dictionary.csv).
- **Interactive dashboard:** open [dashboard/dist/index.html](dashboard/dist/index.html) locally, keeping its folder together.

The summary notebooks show the full merchant fee ranking, the overall top 100 and the top 10 from each of five existing segment portfolios. Segment top-10 lists are views of the selected 100-member portfolios. The technical summary uses the 100% return baseline; the dashboard opens at 99% retention. Both settings are identified in the summaries.

## Fraud adjustment and findings

The saved portfolios use a **30 September 2022** cutoff. Expected monthly fees average March 2021–September 2022; risk uses the 26 complete weeks from 28 March–25 September 2022. All monetary amounts are AUD.

```text
adjusted fee = dollar_value × take_rate / 100 × (1 − consumer_fraud_probability / 100)
```

Selection balances variation in weekly fees (CV) and customer concentration (HHI), with equal weight, while retaining a chosen proportion of the highest-fee portfolio's expected return. The normalized risk index has a baseline of 100. Full definitions and eligibility rules are in [12_summary.ipynb](code/notebooks/12_summary.ipynb).

| Global retention | Expected monthly adjusted fees | Weekly CV | Customer HHI | Risk index | Within requested optimization tolerance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 100% | A$1,551,710.02 | 3.138% | 0.00004583 | 100.000 | Yes |
| 99.5% | A$1,544,100.83 | 3.102% | 0.00004548 | 99.061 | Yes |
| 99% | A$1,536,392.77 | 3.088% | 0.00004527 | 98.601 | Yes |
| 98% | A$1,520,697.99 | 3.032% | 0.00004537 | 97.817 | Yes |
| 95% | A$1,474,508.54 | 2.934% | 0.00004530 | 96.168 | No |

The final exports contain 30 portfolios: the global scope and five segments, each at five retention settings. Sixteen meet the requested 0.1% optimization-gap tolerance; fourteen are the best solutions found with recorded bounds. Search results can vary with environment and budget; use the saved exports to reproduce the delivered lists exactly.

These are retrospective results on synthetic data. Fraud-adjusted fees are a modelled revenue measure, not net profit. Most fraud scores are imputed, and 96.36% of orders in the adjusted run fall outside observed predictor support. Numerical optimization certification does not establish future performance. The notebooks explain these limitations and the treatment of missing data and outliers.

Use the explicit `*_adjusted_fees` fields in the [adjusted exports](handoff/fraud_adjusted/final/). Legacy `*_gross_fees` aliases there also contain adjusted values; files under `handoff/final/` use the original unadjusted basis. Portfolio totals repeat on merchant rows: sum merchant fees within one portfolio and take one copy of its portfolio-level totals.

## Research comparisons

### ABS momentum comparison

[Notebook 02 ABS](code/notebooks/02_abs_momentum_comparison.ipynb) tests whether published retail spending momentum improves merchant forecasts. The [fixed category mapping](tables/reference/abs_category_mapping.csv) records 13 mapped and 12 unmapped categories and their assumptions. The notebook builds its study when completed results are absent.

### External data comparison

[Notebook 03](code/notebooks/03_external_data_comparison.ipynb) compares CPI, business exits and Google search features using the [external category mapping](tables/reference/external_category_mapping.csv). Its prerequisite stages appear in the rebuild commands. Google Trends was not evaluable in the recorded study because the required exports were unavailable. External features were not adopted in the final portfolio score.

### Forecast and TimesFM comparisons

`forecast_comparison.py` compares repeat sales, regression, pooled lag features and damped trends. The optional `selection_headroom.py` measures hindsight ceilings; the original 1% improvement threshold exceeded the available headroom in this dataset.

The optional TimesFM model requires the separate `code/requirements-timesfm.txt` environment, configured CUDA hardware and a local checkpoint under `tables/landing/timesfm3/checkpoint/`. After `prepare`, its commands are `python code/scripts/timesfm_comparison.py smoke` and `python code/scripts/timesfm_comparison.py run`. The model postdates the historical evaluation period, so this is a retrospective experiment. Preserve the checkpoint's upstream license and documentation.

### Customer-concentration and risk studies

Notebooks 04–09 explain the risk and diversification comparisons. The concentration script supports `prepare`, `select`, `evaluate`, `oracle` and `report`; `all` runs those stages. `oracle` uses hindsight. The Windows serial launcher enforces platform-specific resource limits; the standard macOS/Linux entry point can parallelize dates and does not provide those same limits.
